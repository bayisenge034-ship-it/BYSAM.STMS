Chemistry

Study notes, calculations and practical guidance for secondary-school Chemistry. Relative atomic masses used: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, Ca = 40. Molar volume of a gas at room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.) = 24 dm³.

Printable copy: Download Chemistry notes + exercises (PDF)

1. Atoms and the periodic table

An atom has a nucleus of protons (+1) and neutrons (0) surrounded by electrons (−1) in shells. The atomic number = number of protons; the mass number = protons + neutrons. Electrons fill shells 2, 8, 8 …; sodium (11) is 2,8,1.

  • Group number = outer-shell electrons; period number = number of shells.
  • Group 1 (alkali metals) get more reactive down the group; Group 7 (halogens) get less reactive down the group; Group 0 (noble gases) are unreactive because their outer shells are full.

3. The mole and calculations

n = m ÷ M (moles = mass ÷ molar mass)   n = c × V (V in dm³)   n = V ÷ 24 (gas volume in dm³ at r.t.p.)
% by mass of an element = (mass of element in formula ÷ Mr) × 100
Worked examples
Molar mass: H₂SO₄ = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98. In 4.9 g: n = 4.9/98 = 0.05 mol.
Reacting masses: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. From 10 g CaCO₃ (M = 100): n = 0.1 mol, so CO₂ = 0.1 mol = 0.1 × 44 = 4.4 g, volume = 0.1 × 24 = 2.4 dm³.
Concentration: 5.85 g NaCl (M = 58.5) in 500 cm³: n = 0.1 mol, c = 0.1 ÷ 0.5 = 0.2 mol/dm³.
% composition: MgO: 24/40 × 100 = 60% Mg.

Always balance the equation first: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O; Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. The coefficients give the mole ratio.

4. Acids, bases and salts

  • Acids release H⁺ in water; alkalis release OH⁻. pH 1–6 acidic, 7 neutral, 8–14 alkaline. pH = −log[H⁺]: [H⁺] = 0.001 mol/dm³ gives pH 3.
  • Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen. Acid + base → salt + water. Acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide.
  • Neutralisation: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.
Titration calculation
25.0 cm³ of NaOH is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ HCl (1 : 1).
n(HCl) = 0.0200 × 0.100 = 0.00200 mol = n(NaOH). c(NaOH) = 0.00200 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0800 mol/dm³.

5. Rates of reaction and energy changes

Reaction rate increases with higher concentration, higher temperature, larger surface area (smaller particles) and a catalyst, because particles collide more often and/or with more energy. Exothermic reactions release heat (temperature rises); endothermic reactions absorb heat.

6. Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electricity to decompose an ionic compound that is molten or dissolved. Positive ions go to the cathode (reduction); negative ions go to the anode (oxidation). Remember PANIC: Positive Anode, Negative Is Cathode – in electrolysis the anode is connected to the positive terminal of the power supply.

  • Molten lead(II) bromide: lead at the cathode, bromine at the anode.
  • Concentrated sodium chloride solution: hydrogen at the cathode, chlorine at the anode, NaOH left in solution.

9. Practical activities

  • Rate investigation: react marble chips with dilute HCl and collect gas in a syringe; compare rates at different concentrations or temperatures and plot volume against time.
  • Titration: rinse the burette with the solution it will hold, add indicator, add acid slowly near the end-point, and repeat until two titres agree within 0.10 cm³.
  • Salt preparation: add excess insoluble base (copper(II) oxide) to warm dilute sulfuric acid, filter off the excess, evaporate part of the filtrate and crystallise copper(II) sulfate.
  • Safety: wear eye protection, handle acids carefully, and never smell gases directly.

10. Exercises with answers

  1. Calculate the relative formula mass of Ca(OH)₂.
    Answer40 + 2(16 + 1) = 74.
  2. How many moles are in 11 g of CO₂, and what volume does it occupy at r.t.p.?
    Answern = 11/44 = 0.25 mol; volume = 0.25 × 24 = 6 dm³.
  3. What mass of magnesium gives 20 g of magnesium oxide (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO)?
    Answern(MgO) = 20/40 = 0.5 mol = n(Mg); mass = 0.5 × 24 = 12 g.
  4. 4.0 g of NaOH (M = 40) is dissolved to make 250 cm³ of solution. Find the concentration.
    Answern = 0.10 mol; c = 0.10/0.250 = 0.40 mol/dm³.
  5. Name the products at each electrode when molten lead(II) bromide is electrolysed.
    AnswerCathode (−): lead. Anode (+): bromine.
  6. Find the pH of 0.01 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.
    AnswerHCl is a strong acid, so [H⁺] = 0.01 = 10⁻², pH = 2.
  7. Balance: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃.
    Answer4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃.

11. Examination tips

  • Include state symbols and balance every equation. Learn the standard tests and the exact observation words (e.g. “white precipitate”, not “solid”).
  • In mole calculations, write each step: moles → ratio → moles of wanted substance → mass or volume.
  • Use correct names: “particles collide more frequently”, not “they collide more”.
