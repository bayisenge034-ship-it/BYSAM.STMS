Physics

Notes, worked calculations, diagrams, practical activities and exam preparation for secondary-school Physics. In calculations we use g = 10 m/s² unless stated (some syllabuses use 9.8 – check yours).

Printable copy: Download Physics notes + exercises (PDF)

1. Measurement and units

Physics describes the world with measurable quantities. Use SI units: length (m), mass (kg), time (s), current (A), temperature (K or °C).

  • Always convert first: 25 cm = 0.25 m; 2 kW = 2000 W; 90 km/h = 25 m/s (divide by 3.6).
  • Give answers to a sensible number of significant figures (usually the least precise given value).

2. Motion in a straight line

v = u + at   s = ut + ½at²   v² = u² + 2as   s = ½(u + v)t
u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement (constant acceleration only).
Worked example
A car accelerates uniformly from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 8 s. Find its acceleration and the distance covered.
a = (v − u)/t = (25 − 5)/8 = 2.5 m/s²
s = ½(u + v)t = ½(5 + 25)(8) = 120 m

Free fall: ignoring air resistance, all objects accelerate downward at g ≈ 10 m/s². For a ball thrown upward at 12 m/s, the maximum height is v²/(2g) = 144/20 = 7.2 m.

3. Forces and Newton's laws

F = ma (resultant force)   W = mg (weight)   p = F/A (pressure)
  1. First law: an object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts.
  2. Second law: resultant force = mass × acceleration.
  3. Third law: forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different objects.
Worked example
A 1200 kg car is driven with a net forward force of 3000 N. a = F/m = 3000/1200 = 2.5 m/s². A 60 kg student has weight W = mg = 60 × 10 = 600 N (mass stays 60 kg on the Moon; weight changes).

Remember: the resultant force is what counts. Pushing with 20 N against friction of 5 N on a 5 kg block gives (20 − 5)/5 = 3 m/s².

4. Work, energy and power

W = Fd   KE = ½mv²   GPE = mgh   P = W/t = E/t
Energy is conserved: it changes form but the total stays the same.
Worked example
A 2 kg ball is dropped from 20 m. GPE at the top = 2 × 10 × 20 = 400 J. Just before landing all of it is KE: ½(2)v² = 400 → v = 20 m/s.
A pump lifts 600 kg of water through 10 m in 60 s: work = mgh = 600 × 10 × 10 = 60 000 J; power = 60 000/60 = 1000 W. Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100%.

5. Electricity

V = IR   P = VI   E = Pt   Q = It
Series: RT = R₁ + R₂ + …   Parallel: 1/RT = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
Worked examples
Series: 4 Ω and 6 Ω across 12 V. R = 10 Ω, I = 12/10 = 1.2 A, voltage across the 4 Ω resistor = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 V.
Parallel: 6 Ω and 3 Ω: 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, so R = 2 Ω; from 12 V the cell supplies 12/2 = 6 A.
Power: a 60 W lamp on 240 V draws I = P/V = 0.25 A, so R = V/I = 960 Ω.

Safety: fuses and earth wires protect people and appliances. A fuse should be rated just above the normal current.

6. Waves and light

v = fλ   f = 1/T   Refraction: n = sin i / sin r
  • Transverse waves (light, water ripples): oscillations at right angles to travel direction. Longitudinal waves (sound): oscillations along the direction of travel, with compressions and rarefactions.
  • Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Sound needs a medium; light does not.
Worked example
A sound has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.68 m: v = fλ = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s.
Light enters glass (n = 1.5) at i = 45°: sin r = sin 45°/1.5 = 0.4714, so r = 28.1°.

7. Heat and thermal physics

Q = mcΔT   Q = mL (change of state)
c = specific heat capacity (water: 4200 J/kg·°C)
Worked example
Heating 2 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C needs Q = 2 × 4200 × 50 = 420 000 J. A 2 kW heater (100% efficient) takes 420 000/2000 = 210 s.

Heat travels by conduction (particles passing on vibrations, best in metals), convection (moving fluid) and radiation (infrared waves, needs no medium).

8. Practical activities

A. Measure g with a simple pendulum

Apparatus: string, small mass, stand, stopwatch, metre rule. Set length L = 1.00 m, displace by a small angle (under 10°) and time 20 oscillations; T = total time ÷ 20. Then g = 4π²L/T².

Sample result: if T = 2.0 s, g = 39.48 × 1.00 ÷ 4.0 = 9.87 m/s². Improve accuracy: time many swings, repeat and average, measure L to the centre of the mass, start timing at the centre of a swing.

B. Investigate Ohm's law

Connect a cell, ammeter (series), voltmeter (parallel) and a resistor. Vary the voltage (change the number of cells or use a rheostat), record V and I, plot V against I. A straight line through the origin shows V ∝ I; the gradient is R. Safety: keep currents small so the wire does not heat.

C. Specific heat capacity of water

Heat a measured mass of water in an insulated container with an electric heater of known power. Record the temperature rise ΔT over time t. c = Pt/(mΔT). Lagging and a lid reduce heat loss, the main source of error.

9. Exercises with answers

Try each question first, then open the answer.

  1. A train starts from rest and accelerates at 0.5 m/s² for 40 s. Find its final speed and the distance travelled.
    Answerv = 0 + 0.5 × 40 = 20 m/s. s = ½at² = ½ × 0.5 × 1600 = 400 m.
  2. A 5 kg block is pushed with 20 N while friction is 5 N. Find its acceleration.
    AnswerResultant = 15 N, a = 15/5 = 3 m/s².
  3. A 0.5 kg stone is thrown upward at 12 m/s. What maximum height does it reach? (g = 10 m/s²)
    Answerh = v²/2g = 144/20 = 7.2 m. (Mass does not matter.)
  4. Find the combined resistance of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω in parallel.
    Answer1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 1, so R = 1 Ω.
  5. A 2500 W kettle works from 230 V. What current does it draw?
    AnswerI = P/V = 2500/230 ≈ 10.9 A, so a 13 A fuse would be suitable.
  6. A radio wave has frequency 100 MHz and speed 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Find its wavelength.
    Answerλ = v/f = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 1.0 × 10⁸ = 3.0 m.
  7. How much heat is needed to warm 0.5 kg of copper (c = 390 J/kg·°C) from 20 °C to 100 °C?
    AnswerQ = 0.5 × 390 × 80 = 15 600 J.

10. Examination tips

  • Write the formula, substitute with units, then give the answer with a unit – method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong.
  • Convert units before calculating. Check answers are sensible (a car cannot accelerate at 500 m/s²).
  • For “explain” questions, link the cause to the effect using key terms (for example, “greater force → greater acceleration because a = F/m”).
  • Label graph axes with quantity and unit; use most of the grid; draw a best-fit line, not join-the-dots.
